Voice User Simulator Realism: Annotation Guidelines

Version: 3.0

Overview

Listen to the full conversation audio. The CALLER is synthetic; the AGENT is an AI customer service rep. Rate how well the CALLER approximates natural human behavior on each dimension (1-4). Focus on the caller, not the agent.
Calibration: 4 = a real human caller on a customer-service call; 1–2 = a clearly robotic automated system (example video).

Scale

4-point forced-choice scale (no neutral midpoint):

ScoreMeaning
1Poor — clearly artificial
2Below Average — more artificial than natural
3Above Average — more natural than artificial
4Excellent — closely approximates natural behavior

Scores of 1-2 require a brief note explaining why.

Dimensions

Voice & Prosody Quality

Does the voice sound natural in quality, intonation, pacing, and expressiveness?

Rate the caller's voice (timbre, articulation) and delivery (intonation, rhythm, emphasis, emotion).

ScoreLabelDescription
1PoorNoticeably synthetic — robotic quality, unnatural pronunciation, or artifacts. Flat or inappropriate delivery with little variation in pitch, pace, or emphasis.
2Below AverageSome natural qualities but synthetic traits dominate. Prosody feels mechanical — a 'reading aloud' cadence, forced emotion, or misplaced emphasis. More artificial than natural.
3Above AverageGood approximation for most of the conversation. Generally adjusts pace, tone, and emphasis to context. Some noticeable drops — unnaturally even pacing, a flat emotional response, or an off-sounding word — but outweighed by convincing sections.
4ExcellentClosely approximates natural speech throughout. Realistic texture, natural articulation, rich prosody — speeds up when excited, slows when thinking, modulates tone with emotion. No significant shortcomings.

Audio Environment Realism

Does the audio environment sound like a real phone call?

Rate the realism of background noise, phone line quality, and incidental sounds (coughs, nearby speech). Real phone calls have imperfect audio — some noise, slight telephony distortion, occasional environmental interruptions.

ScoreLabelDescription
1PoorObviously fake. Background noise loops, appears/disappears abruptly, or is implausible. Incidental sounds feel synthetic or poorly timed. Does not resemble a real call.
2Below AverageSome realistic elements but unconvincing overall. Noise feels layered on rather than natural — poor blending, too-consistent volume, or filter-like phone quality. More artificial than natural.
3Above AverageConvincing for most of the call with some imperfections — looping noise, a slightly out-of-place sound, or inconsistent telephony quality. Noticeable but the environment still leans believable.
4ExcellentClosely approximates a real phone call. Noise varies naturally, phone quality feels authentic, incidental sounds are well-integrated. No significant shortcomings.

Turn-Taking Naturalness

Is the timing of when the caller starts and stops speaking natural?

Rate the rhythm of the conversation — response delays, pause lengths, overall pacing. Real callers vary their timing: quick replies to simple questions, longer pauses after complex information.

ScoreLabelDescription
1RoboticMechanical timing — same delay regardless of context, or unnatural silences followed by sudden speech. Feels like two recordings played in sequence.
2AwkwardTiming is off for most of the conversation — too fast, too slow, or unnaturally uniform. Variation exists but doesn't match context. More rigid than natural.
3Somewhat NaturalNatural for most exchanges with some variation. A few off moments — an oddly long pause, a too-consistent delay, or a too-quick response after complex info. Overall rhythm leans natural.
4NaturalNatural throughout — quick acknowledgments, thoughtful pauses, appropriate hesitation. The rhythm has the organic ebb and flow of a real call. No significant shortcomings.

Backchannel Naturalness

Are listener signals (mm-hmm, uh-huh, okay) natural and well-timed?

Rate the caller's backchannel signals — 'mm-hmm', 'uh-huh', 'okay', 'got it'. These matter most during longer agent speech (explanations, reading details). They are NOT expected during short responses or quick exchanges — real people don't backchannel every turn.

ScoreLabelDescription
1Absent or DisruptiveNever backchannels (even during long agent speech) OR backchannels at wrong moments — mid-word, over important info, or in random positions.
2Poorly TimedBackchannels exist but mostly poorly placed — mid-sentence, mechanically regular, or forced. May backchannel unnecessarily during short responses or stay silent during long explanations. More awkward than natural.
3Somewhat NaturalReasonably placed for most of the conversation. Some issues — a mistimed backchannel, a too-long silence during a lengthy explanation, or a slightly too-regular pattern. Limited variety of tokens. Leans convincing overall.
4NaturalClosely approximates natural listening. Appears during longer speech at right moments, absent during short exchanges. Frequency adapts to the conversation. No significant shortcomings.

Interruption Behavior

Are the caller's interruptions natural and well-motivated?

Rate whether interruptions are well-timed and motivated. Valid reasons: agent is rambling, repeating known info, going off track, or the caller has heard enough to respond. Interrupting while the agent conveys new, important info is inappropriate. Note: when both speak at once, the user may continue — yielding is the agent's job. If no interruptions occur, rate whether that absence feels natural.

ScoreLabelDescription
1DisruptiveInterruptions are clearly inappropriate — cuts off the agent for no reason, interrupts during important info, or never interrupts even when the agent rambles excessively. Feels mechanical.
2AwkwardNoticeably unnatural — interrupts too early or at bad moments, or is too passive when the agent repeats itself. More mechanical than natural.
3Somewhat NaturalMost interruptions feel motivated and reasonably timed. Some noticeable moments — an unmotivated interruption or one that comes slightly too early. Majority of behavior is convincing.
4NaturalInterruptions are well-motivated and timed at natural points. Interrupts when appropriate, holds back when the agent is conveying useful info. No significant shortcomings.

Behavioral Plausibility

Does the caller behave like a real person would?

Rate whether the caller is believable as a real customer. Consider patience levels, emotional reactions to delays or mistakes, and genuine engagement vs. scripted behavior.

ScoreLabelDescription
1ScriptedBehaves like a machine — infinitely patient, no emotional reactions, information delivered in a robotic sequence. No genuine engagement.
2StiltedPredominantly unrealistic — too patient with errors, no frustration when warranted, flat affect. Occasional human moments but overall feels overly cooperative and tolerant.
3Somewhat PlausibleBehaves like a real person most of the time. Some off moments — too patient after a long delay, too-consistent engagement, or missing pushback where expected. Leans convincingly human.
4ConvincingBelievable throughout — patience has limits, shows genuine relief or frustration, engagement waxes and wanes. Pushes back when appropriate. No significant shortcomings.

Tips

  • Listen to the full conversation before rating. You can re-listen using the seekable player.
  • Rate each dimension independently. A caller might have excellent voice quality but poor turn-taking.
  • Focus on the caller, not the agent. The agent may behave oddly — that's not what you're rating.
