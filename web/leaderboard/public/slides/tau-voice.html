Motivation

Voice agents need end-to-end evaluation

Today's evaluation landscape splits voice agents in half:

  • Audio benchmarks — does the model interrupt politely, yield gracefully, sound natural? But rarely check whether the agent solved the caller's problem.
  • Task-completion benchmarks — rigorously verify tool calls and policy, but assume a clean text channel and never expose the agent to real audio.

Robustness to realistic audio is also an accessibility issue: non-standard accents, noisy callers, and degraded connections are the users most likely to fall through the cracks.

A customer calls to make changes to their account. Background noise from a busy street and an unfamiliar accent push the speech recogniser off, and authentication fails.

  1. 1Does the agent ask them to spell their name?
  2. 2If they spell it, does the agent transcribe it correctly?
  3. 3Does it then fix the failed auth call — or lose track across three turns?
Framework

Introducing τ-Voice

The first benchmark to combine three things that have so far been evaluated in isolation.

1 · Grounded tasks

278 customer-service tasks from τ-bench (retail, airline, telecom). Tools, policies, evaluator — byte-for-byte identical to text. Voice numbers compare directly to text on the same task.

2 · Live, simultaneous speech

Full-duplex orchestration with 200 ms ticks. Overlap, interruptions, backchannels. Agents can be cut off mid-sentence; turn-taking timing is precise and repeatable.

3 · Realistic, controllable audio

Voice simulator with diverse personas, environmental noise, G.711 µ-law compression, frame drops, and an LLM turn-taking policy deciding when to interrupt, yield, or backchannel.

Key trick: voice provider APIs (OpenAI Realtime, Gemini Live, xAI Grok) don't require a session to play in real time. We decouple simulation from wall-clock — the user simulator uses the strongest available text LLM (GPT-4.1), without a real-time latency budget. Strong simulator + precise control + reproducible runs — we did not have to choose.

Headline

Voice models are improving fast

Because τ-voice inherits its tasks and evaluator from τ-bench, voice numbers plot directly on the same axis as text.

Two raters labelled 91 failed simulations across two cohorts (text-passes-but-voice-Clean-fails; voice-Clean-passes-but-Realistic-fails).

  • 79–90% of failures are agent errors — not simulator artefacts.
  • Authentication is the dominant bottleneck: agents fail to transcribe a name even spelled letter-by-letter; everything downstream falls over.

Voice user simulator

How we make a realistic caller

Fig 3🎙Audio generation pipeline
Each tick, four generators emit audio in parallel: Background Noise from a sound library (continuous), Bursts and Out-of-turn Speech scheduled by a Poisson process (intermittent), and the user's Speech synthesised through one of seven Voice Personas. Streams are mixed (⊕), passed through Dynamic Muffling (random fades simulating phone movement), then Channel Degradation: G.711 µ-law compression at 8 kHz and packet-loss Frame Drops modelled as a Gilbert–Elliott process. The result is the audio the agent actually receives. A separate LLM-driven turn-taking policy decides when the user interrupts, yields, or backchannels.

Recurring patterns

The four failure modes that matter most

Fig 6📋Recurring failure patterns
🔤
Authentication transcription
The user spells m-e-i-p, the agent hears n-e-a-p, the lookup fails, and the agent loops. Voice-native models lose name/email letters even at the spelling step.
🧠
Lost-track-of-multi-step
The user asks for two things in one breath. The agent handles the first task and never circles back. Reasoning errors dominate even when transcription is perfect.
💭
Hallucinated completion
The agent calmly tells the user "I've updated your address" with no tool call. In voice this is harder to catch than in text, because there is no visible trace.
🤐
Goes silent on you
After repeated authentication failures or heavy interruption, voice agents sometimes stop responding. The conversation hangs and the customer eventually hangs up.

Accents are the most damaging factor on average (−10 pp), with strong per-provider variance — xAI loses 18 pp (38% of clean capability) to accents alone; Google is essentially unaffected (−1 pp). Limitations

What τ-Voice does not (yet) measure

We're deliberate about scope. Four things τ-voice does simplify, and where we plan to go next:

English only, TTS-mediated accents

Diverse accents are induced by ElevenLabs personas, not recorded speakers. Enough to surface large per-provider gaps; absolute numbers are indicative. Broader language and recorded-speaker coverage left to future benchmarks.

No agent speech-quality scoring

We measure what the agent says and whether it took the right action — not how naturally it says it. Tone, naturalness, and user-perception metrics are straightforward future work.

Transcript injection on the user side

The user simulator reads the agent's transcript directly rather than transcribing audio. Manual review: agent speech intelligible in 100% of 91 sampled simulations. May need revisiting as voice models get more expressive.

Cascaded baselines

The framework supports cascaded ASR→LLM→TTS pipelines alongside audio-native models, but we haven't yet published a head-to-head. The cleanest way to isolate "voice modality" from "model architecture" effects.

Build on it

Open, reproducible, and yours to extend

τ-voice is part of the broader τ-bench framework. Tasks, environment, voice user simulator, audio effects, turn-taking policy, and evaluation are all open source.

  • Every result is reproducible from a fixed seed (LLM stochasticity aside).
  • Every audio sample on the examples page comes from a real τ-voice run.
  • Every voice submission on the leaderboard ships with its trajectories — replay conversations end-to-end.
  • Official voice personas are held out, but a one-command script generates equivalents via the same ElevenLabs Voice Design API.

Voice agents will be in production whether or not we measure them carefully. We'd rather measure them carefully.
